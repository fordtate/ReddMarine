## Vision

### 10x Check
The product isn't the dock box — it's the **operating system for waterfront property
infrastructure**. Dock power is the wedge. The platform is dock power + boat lift +
shore power + dock cameras + gate + water-level/weather, under one app and one
installer relationship: "the electrical panel for the water." MyDockLink is stuck at
lift control; nobody owns the whole waterfront. This is the cathedral version — and
also a two-year detour if pursued now, which is why it is a headroom decision (D5),
not a v1 commitment.

## Premise Challenge (findings)

1. **Business shape is unexamined.** The doc defaults to homeowner SaaS ($15/mo), but
   the economics (~$5.75/mo gross per dock before opex) and the small annual market of
   high-end private docks fit a regional electrical-infrastructure / trade business
   (hardware margin + installed base + builder service contracts) better than a
   venture-scale SaaS. Different funding, staffing, exit. → **Resolved into scope (D2).**
2. **Proxy risk: the durable compounding asset is "installer of record with N docks on
   the system," not "homeowner has an active subscription."** The metric that matters
   is **docks-per-builder and builder retention**, not homeowner MRR. → **Folded into
   D2's accepted scope:** docks-per-builder and builder retention become the north-star
   metric, added to the 90-day success criteria; homeowner renewal stays a gate but is
   not the headline number.
3. **Inversion — ranked failure modes and disposition:**
   a. Builders won't chase renewals ($3.75/mo royalty beneath their notice) — the
      channel thesis is hollow. #1 risk. → **D3 partially mitigates** (builder gets
      value on dock #1 regardless of the royalty); **the 30-day demand gate is the
      actual test.**
   b. A liability event — injury/death at a monitored dock, founder named in the suit
      regardless of disclaimers. Ends a pre-revenue company. → **Resolved into scope (D4).**
   c. MyDockLink adds leakage monitoring in a quarter and out-distributes via dealers.
      → **Partially mitigated by D3** (breadth of builder-service value they don't have).
      D5 is not a v1 build and does not help inside the validation window. Otherwise
      **accepted as a live competitive risk** — being first with the builder
      relationship is the only real defense.
   d. Regulation cuts the other way — a residential dock GFP mandate is won by the
      certified breaker makers, monitoring becomes an optional add-on. → **Monitor.**
      Not actionable pre-revenue; if it starts moving, the insurer-discount channel
      (deferred item) becomes the priority pivot.
   e. Per-dock unit economics never turn positive. → **Accepted as the core open risk**;
      the design doc's Working Assumptions name the levers (higher price, capped
      royalty, cheaper connectivity, scale). The 30-day builder interviews test which
      lever the channel will bear.

## Accepted Scope (added to this plan)

- **D2 — Business Shape.** New section in the design doc. The 30-day builder-interview
  script now asks builders directly: resell a subscription for a cut, or buy the kit
  at a margin and run their own service plan? Their answer picks SaaS vs trade-business
  vs hybrid. **North-star metric shifts to docks-per-builder + builder retention**
  (added to the 90-day success criteria); homeowner year-two renewal stays a 30-day
  gate but is no longer the headline number.
  - **What changes under each answer:** *SaaS* → the doc as written stands. *Hybrid*
    → same, with a "buy at margin" option added to the builder agreement. *Trade
    business* → the royalty/subscription model, the unit-economics section, and the
    90-day pricing work all need rework: the homeowner sub becomes optional, revenue
    shifts to kit margin + a builder platform fee, and the north-star becomes
    active-builder count. **A clear trade-business answer triggers a design-doc
    revision before any 90-day work starts.**
- **D3 — Builder service dashboard in v1.** Service-request queue, per-dock history
  (installs, alerts, visits), warranty/install log. **Concrete boundary:** one status
  field per service request; no scheduling, dispatch, routing, or SLA tracking — that
  is a later platform build, not this.
  - **Named dependency:** a **minimal single-builder login** (email + password, one
    builder org, no team roles). This is the auth carve-out that keeps "builder
    accounts" (still deferred) out of scope while letting the dashboard exist.
  - **Timing:** built only if the 30-day demand gate passes. Not now.
- **D4 — Liability, staged.**
  - *In the 30-day window:* form the business entity; open conversations with 2-3
    product-liability brokers and get at least a **written risk-appetite response**
    (firm quotes routinely lag for a pre-revenue, pre-cert, drowning-adjacent product —
    a slow quote is not a kill signal, a "we won't touch this" is).
  - *Before the first on-dock unit:* full counsel-drafted ToS + disclaimer + homeowner
    install sign-off. Ask counsel specifically whether the "trend alerting" feature
    changes the liability picture versus passive logging.
  - *Pre-entity money:* the $50 homeowner deposits and any builder pilot fees collected
    before the entity exists are held refundable in a dedicated account (or collected
    as a sole proprietor pending formation) — confirm the clean path with counsel at
    entity formation.
- **D5 — Platform-ready headroom.** Not a v1 build task — a **constraint carried into
  /plan-eng-review**: spare I/O channel count and a generic `device` / `channel` cloud
  schema, so the waterfront-OS path is a config change, not a respin. No boat-lift /
  camera / gate / water-level vertical in v1.

## NOT in scope

- Any second infrastructure vertical (lift, cameras, gate) in v1.
- Stripe billing / automated royalty payout (deferred until first real renewal —
  unchanged from the design doc).
- Full **builder accounts** (team roles, multi-org, permissions) — D3 uses the minimal
  single-builder login only.
- Custom PCB, marine hardening, native mobile apps, OTA (unchanged).
- Scheduling / dispatch / SLA tracking in the builder dashboard.
